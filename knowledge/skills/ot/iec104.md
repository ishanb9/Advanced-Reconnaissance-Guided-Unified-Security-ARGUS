# IEC 60870-5-104 guidance

IEC 60870-5-104 (IEC 104) is the TCP/IP adaptation of the IEC 60870-5-101 serial telecontrol standard. It runs exclusively on TCP/2404 and is the dominant SCADA communication protocol for power-grid substations, water-treatment facilities, and pipeline supervisory systems. A master station (control centre) opens a persistent TCP connection to a Remote Terminal Unit (RTU) or gateway, exchanges Application Protocol Control Information (APCI) frames, and then sends Application Service Data Units (ASDUs) to read process values or issue control commands to field devices.

During an authorized penetration test, the primary goal is to enumerate what is exposed without altering plant state. Begin by completing the STARTDT handshake (U-format frame 0x68 0x04 0x07 0x00 0x00 0x00) to confirm the service is live and to fingerprint APCI parameters (k, w, t0, t1, t2, t3). Follow with an nmap NSE scan using the iec104-info script, which safely parses the connection acknowledgement and any spontaneous (COT=3) data the RTU transmits. Record all TypeIDs observed in I-format frames; a mix of measurement types (TypeID 1-21) alongside command types (TypeID 45-70) reveals that control plane and monitoring plane share the same unauthenticated channel — a critical finding.

The key risk is the complete absence of authentication in pre-IEC 62351-5 deployments. Any host that can reach TCP/2404 can issue General Interrogation (TypeID 100) to retrieve the entire process image, or worse, send Single Command (C_SC_NA_1, TypeID 45) or Double Command (C_DC_NA_1, TypeID 46) ASDUs to operate breakers, valves, or pump motors. Network-layer access control (firewalls, unidirectional security gateways, or application-layer proxies) is frequently absent because operational continuity is prioritised over segmentation. Exposure of IEC 104 directly to a corporate LAN or the internet is an immediately critical finding.

All write/control quick wins above are gated disruptive actions and must not be executed without explicit engagement-window approval, field team standby, and confirmation that the target IOA maps to a test point rather than a live load. Remediation priorities are: deploy IEC 62351-5 authentication, isolate 2404/tcp behind a data-diode or industrial DMZ, enable sequence-number monitoring for replay-attack detection, and transition to authenticated protocol variants or VPN encapsulation for remote SCADA links.
